The integration of a controller, gate driver, current regulator, and Power FET into a single chip for USB Power Delivery (PD) solutions is of paramount importance in the context of modern electronics. As devices become increasingly compact and power-hungry, efficient power management and delivery are critical. However, current solutions often face significant challenges, including thermal dissipation, IR drops, and electro-migration, which can compromise performance and reliability. These issues arise due to the high current densities and complex interconnections required in traditional multi-chip designs. 

\vspace{10pt}

The proposed design aims to reduce the overall footprint, improve thermal management, and enhance the reliability of USB PD solutions, paving the way for more efficient and compact power delivery systems.

\vspace{10pt}

The primary objectives of this project are to design and implement a USB PD 3.1 compliant controller using CMOS technology, and a USB PD 3.1 compliant power supply using BCD technology. Additionally, the project aims to integrate these components and evaluate the performance metrics of the designed power supply, such as transient response and stability. The proposed controller will be compared with existing designs in terms of performance and area.

\vspace{10pt}

Various test cases were designed to evaluate the performance under different load conditions and input voltages. The results demonstrated that the design successfully delivers 100W to the output while consuming 110.3W from the DC input, with 8W attributed to heat dissipation. This significant enhancement underscores the efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed solution.
